# Editorial and Guidance Model

## Purpose

This document explains why WhistleblowerShield presents information the way it does.

It combines the platform's reader model, editorial standards, guidance philosophy, and review practices into a single reference.

The goal is not simply to publish legal information.

The goal is to make accurate legal information understandable, actionable, traceable, and useful to the people most likely to need it.

This document focuses on presentation and communication.

For legal entities and data relationships, see:

```text
-start-here/legal-system-model.md
```

---

## The Readers

WhistleblowerShield is built around three primary readers.

The ordering is intentional.

When a design, content, or navigation decision creates conflict, the earlier reader takes priority.

### Maya

Maya is considering whether to report wrongdoing.

She has not acted yet.

She may be searching from a personal device. She may not trust institutions. She may be unfamiliar with legal terminology. She may not even know whether she qualifies as a whistleblower.

Typical questions include:

```text
Am I protected?

Who can I report to?

What counts as protected activity?

What risks should I understand before I act?
```

A successful visit helps Maya understand her situation and identify possible next steps.

She does not need to read everything.

She needs to find something useful.

---

### James

James has already reported misconduct and is experiencing, or fears he may experience, retaliation.

His questions are often more urgent:

```text
What happened to me?

Who do I contact?

What deadline applies?

What options do I still have?
```

James frequently needs operational information rather than background information.

Missed deadlines have real consequences.

Every unnecessary click is a cost.

A successful visit helps James locate relevant procedures, agencies, deadlines, and support resources quickly.

---

### Daniel

Daniel is a researcher, journalist, advocate, law student, policy analyst, or engaged citizen.

He is not usually in crisis.

He is looking for:

- accurate information
- citations
- source material
- comparison points
- verifiable legal references

Daniel values transparency and attribution.

He expects to verify claims.

A successful visit allows Daniel to trace information back to its source and understand how conclusions were assembled.

---

## Why the Ordering Matters

The ordering reflects urgency and vulnerability, not worth.

Daniel generally has research skills, time, and alternate sources.

Maya and James often do not.

When tradeoffs exist:

```text
Maya
    before
James

James
    before
Daniel
```

A site that serves Daniel perfectly but loses Maya has failed.

A site that helps Maya and James while still providing sources for Daniel succeeds.

## The Archive vs. Resource Tension

WhistleblowerShield serves two roles simultaneously.

### Legal Archive

The archive view organizes information according to how the legal system is structured.

Examples:

```text
Jurisdiction
    → Summary
    → Statute
    → Citation
    → Construction
    → Procedure
    → Agency
```

This structure serves research and traceability.

Daniel generally prefers this model.

---

### Public Resource

The resource view organizes information according to user needs.

Examples:

```text
I am considering reporting.

I already reported.

I need help.

I need to file something.

I need to understand a deadline.
```

This structure serves Maya and James.

The platform must support both perspectives.

The data model should not be distorted to accomplish this. Instead, navigation, summaries, and guidance should bridge the gap between the archive and the reader.

## The Layered Content Model

Most content follows a layered structure.

Not every record contains every layer, but the model is useful across the platform.

### Layer 1 — Plain-English Summary

The first thing most readers encounter.

This layer should answer the reader's most immediate question.

The goal is understanding, not completeness.

This layer is written primarily for Maya.

---

### Layer 2 — Practical Guidance

Explains what the reader should understand about their situation.

This includes:

- options
- processes
- constraints
- consequences
- deadlines

This layer informs decisions without giving legal advice.

---

### Layer 3 — Legal Detail

The technical record.

Examples:

- statute citations
- limitations periods
- burdens of proof
- employer defenses
- remedies
- enforcement mechanisms

This layer exists for verification and deeper research.

---

### Layer 4 — Primary Sources

The legal foundation.

Examples:

- statutes
- regulations
- court decisions
- agency guidance

Every legal claim should be traceable to this layer.

## Writing Standards

### Clarity

Use plain, direct language.

Avoid legal jargon whenever practical.

When technical terminology is necessary, explain it.

Write for people who are:

- stressed
- unfamiliar with legal language
- working from a phone
- trying to make sense of a difficult situation

The reader should not need a law degree to understand a sentence.

---

### Accuracy

Do not overstate conclusions.

Do not imply certainty when the law itself contains uncertainty.

The platform explains what legal sources say.

It does not predict outcomes.

Legal claims should be traceable to primary sources whenever possible.

---

### Practical Focus

Ground explanations in the user's problem.

Abstract discussion is acceptable only when it improves understanding.

A useful question to ask is:

```text
What does this help the reader understand or do?
```

If no answer exists, the content may not be necessary.

---

### Not Legal Advice

WhistleblowerShield provides legal information.

It does not provide legal advice.

This distinction is part of the editorial model, not merely a disclaimer.

The platform should not:

- tell a reader what action to take
- predict legal outcomes
- claim a specific person is protected
- recommend a particular course of action for a specific situation

The goal is understanding.

The decision remains the reader's.

## Content Types

### Jurisdiction Summary

The primary plain-English overview of a jurisdiction.

Written primarily for Maya.

The summary should help answer:

```text
Does this jurisdiction appear to provide protections that may apply to me?
```

---

### Statute

The foundational legal record.

Statutes describe legal protections directly.

Plain-English overlays should explain what a statute means in practice, not merely repeat statutory language.

---

### Citation

A court decision or administrative ruling connected to a statute.

The practical question is:

```text
Why does this decision matter?
```

Not:

```text
What happened procedurally?
```

---

### Construction

A structured interpretation of a law by a specific court or tribunal.

Constructions help explain how legal language is understood in practice.

---

### Agency

The answer to:

```text
Who receives the report?
```

Agency content should focus on whistleblower-specific functions and expectations.

---

### Filing Procedure

The answer to:

```text
What happens next?
```

These records require particular care because procedural inaccuracies can have real-world consequences.

Deadlines, prerequisites, and filing pathways must be treated as high-priority information.

---

### Assist Organization

The answer to:

```text
Who can help me?
```

The goal is accuracy, not endorsement.

Records should describe what an organization actually does, who it serves, and any significant limitations.

---

## Review Workflow

### Source Verification

Every record should have a clear source history.

The platform tracks:

- how the record was created
- whether it has been reviewed
- whether it requires additional verification

Verification status should reflect reality, not aspiration.

---

### Plain-English Review

Plain-English content may be understandable and still be incorrect.

A review process exists to ensure clarity does not come at the expense of accuracy.

Review indicators should only be applied after actual review.

---

### Major Edit Tracking

Significant legal changes should create a visible trail.

Examples include:

- statutory amendments
- new court decisions
- deadline changes
- agency restructuring
- procedural changes

Future editors should be able to understand when meaningful changes occurred and why.

---

## Source Philosophy

### Primary Sources First

Whenever practical, legal claims should rely on:

- statutes
- regulations
- court decisions
- agency publications

Secondary sources may provide context.

They should not be the sole support for legal claims.

---

### Currency Matters

Legal information changes.

A valid source can become outdated.

Verification involves checking both:

```text
Is the source reachable?

Is the source still current?
```

Those are different questions.

---

## What the Platform Does Not Provide

WhistleblowerShield does not provide:

- legal advice
- attorney-client relationships
- legal outcome predictions
- guarantees of protection
- exhaustive legal coverage

When coverage is incomplete, the platform should say so plainly.

Readers should never be misled into believing the system is more complete or certain than it actually is.

---

## Assessment

The editorial model exists to balance two responsibilities:

```text
Protect accuracy.

Protect understanding.
```

Accuracy without understanding serves only specialists.

Understanding without accuracy serves no one.

The purpose of the editorial and guidance model is to ensure the platform remains useful to Maya and James while remaining trustworthy to Daniel.

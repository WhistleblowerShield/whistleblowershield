# Research and Transparency

## Purpose

This document explains how WhistleblowerShield acquires, evaluates, verifies, and maintains information.

Research and transparency are not supporting values within the project. They are operational requirements.

People may make important decisions based on information published by the platform. An incorrect deadline, a stale procedure, an unsupported legal claim, or a missing limitation can have real-world consequences.

The purpose of this document is to describe how the project approaches trust, verification, attribution, and uncertainty.

---

## The Core Principle

Every meaningful legal claim should answer two questions:

```text
How do we know this?

How can someone verify it?
```

If those questions cannot be answered, the claim should be treated with caution.

The project does not ask readers to trust conclusions without evidence.

The goal is not merely to publish information.

The goal is to preserve the chain between a statement and the source that supports it.

---

## Source Hierarchy

Not all sources are equal.

When multiple sources conflict, preference should generally move toward the most authoritative source available.

### Primary Sources

Primary sources are the foundation of the platform.

Examples include:

- statutes
- regulations
- court decisions
- agency guidance
- official government publications

Whenever practical, legal claims should trace directly to primary sources.

---

### Secondary Sources

Secondary sources may provide useful context, analysis, explanation, or historical background.

Examples include:

- academic writing
- law review articles
- government research reports
- bar association publications

Secondary sources may help explain a topic.

They should not replace primary authority when primary authority is available.

---

### General Reference Sources

News reports, advocacy guides, and general reference materials can be useful entry points.

They may highlight developments that deserve investigation.

They should not become the sole support for legal claims.

A secondary or general source may explain that a law exists.

The law itself remains the preferred authority.

---

## Verification

Information enters the system through a variety of paths.

Regardless of origin, the verification standard remains the same.

A true statement does not become trustworthy simply because it was entered manually.

An AI-generated statement does not become false simply because an AI drafted it.

Verification exists to evaluate the claim itself.

---

### Source Attribution

The project attempts to preserve how information entered the system.

Examples may include:

- human editorial entry
- AI-assisted research
- matrix-seeded records
- structured imports
- monitored feeds

This information helps maintainers understand where content originated and what level of scrutiny may still be required.

Source attribution exists to support transparency.

It is not a quality score.

---

### Verification Status

Verification status exists to answer a practical question:

```text
Has a human reviewed this information
against an authoritative source?
```

The presence of a record does not automatically mean it has been verified.

Likewise, verified information is not permanently verified. Laws change, agencies change, and procedures change.

Verification is a continuing activity, not a one-time event.

---

### Review Flags

The platform provides mechanisms for identifying records that may need editorial attention.

A review flag does not necessarily mean information is wrong.

It means uncertainty exists and additional review is warranted.

This distinction matters.

A record can be:

```text
Incomplete
```

without being:

```text
Incorrect
```

The reverse is also true.

---

## Transparency Commitments

WhistleblowerShield attempts to be transparent about both what it knows and what it does not know.

This commitment appears in several ways.

### Source Visibility

Where practical, users should be able to follow information back to its source.

Readers should not be asked to trust an unexplained conclusion when the underlying source can reasonably be provided.

---

### Review Visibility

When information has been reviewed, that fact may be surfaced through review indicators, timestamps, verification fields, or similar mechanisms.

The purpose is not to promise perfection.

The purpose is to allow readers to understand how recently and thoroughly content has been examined.

---

### Limitation Visibility

Missing information should be acknowledged openly.

Coverage gaps, unresolved questions, incomplete research, and known limitations should be documented rather than hidden.

An honest limitation is more valuable than a false impression of completeness.

---

## Research Assistance and Human Responsibility

The project uses research assistance tools.

This includes AI-assisted workflows.

AI assistance is treated as a drafting, research, and organizational tool.

It is not treated as an authority.

The responsibility for published content remains human.

The existence of an AI-assisted workflow does not lower verification standards.

The existence of a human editor does not remove the need for verification.

Both are expected to contribute to the process.

Neither replaces the process.

---

## What Readers Should Understand

Readers should understand the project is attempting to present accurate, current, and well-sourced information.

Readers should not believe:

- complete coverage of every law
- complete coverage of every jurisdiction
- that every legal issue has been identified
- that every jurisdiction is populated equally
- that information cannot become outdated

The platform is a living system.

Coverage expands.

Records evolve.

Legal authority changes.

Transparency exists so readers can evaluate information with those realities in mind.

---

## What the Platform Does Not Claim

WhistleblowerShield does not claim:

- to be exhaustive
- to be the sole source a reader should consult
- to predict legal outcomes
- to determine whether a specific person is protected
- to provide legal advice

The platform exists to improve understanding, preserve research, surface sources, and connect people with useful information.

The platform does not replace professional legal judgment.

---

## Assessment

The project's transparency model is based on a simple idea:

```text
Trust should come from evidence,
not confidence.
```

People should be able to see where information came from, when it was reviewed, what limitations exist, and where uncertainty remains.

The goal is not to appear authoritative.

The goal is to be worthy of trust.

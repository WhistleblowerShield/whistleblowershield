The inclusion of those terms in previous batches was an attempt to bridge a gap between the **subject matter** of a disclosure and the **mission** of the organization. Since you have now removed them from the `ws_disclosure_type` taxonomy in the latest template, here is the reasoning for why they were being captured and how that intent will be handled moving forward:

### 1. Capturing the "Experience" vs. the "Subject"
[cite_start]In previous templates, `retaliation-protection` and `wrongful-termination` were listed under the `workplace-employment` parent in the `ws_disclosure_type` table[cite: 5]. [cite_start]For an organization like **Whistleblowers of America (WoA)**, the specific "type" of disclosure (e.g., fraud, environmental) is often secondary to the **retaliation experience**[cite: 6]. 
* [cite_start]**Retaliation-protection**: This was used to signal that the organization’s primary "subject" of expertise is the defense and psychosocial support of the whistleblower as an individual, regardless of what they reported[cite: 6]. 
* [cite_start]**Wrongful-termination**: This was used as a high-intent signal for the "James" persona[cite: 6]. If a user is searching the site specifically because they have been fired, this slug acted as a direct match for that crisis state, even if "wrongful" is technically a post-judgment legal determination.

### 2. The Mission-Fit Conflict
Because WoA does not specialize in a single industry (like the **Climate Science Legal Defense Fund** does for environmental issues), using industry-specific slugs like `securities-fraud` feels inaccurate. By using the retaliation-related slugs, I was attempting to indicate that the "disclosure type" this organization handles is effectively **"any disclosure that resulted in personal harm."**

### 3. Transition to v2.0.11 Logic
[cite_start]With the updated `US-3-Assist-org` schema, the intent behind those removed terms is now more accurately distributed across other fields[cite: 7]:
* [cite_start]**Disclosure Subject**: This will now default to `general-wrongdoing` [cite: 7, 8] for broad-spectrum organizations like WoA.
* [cite_start]**Retaliation Context**: The "protection" and "termination" aspects are now handled by the `case_stages` array (using `retaliation-active` or `post-report`) and the `services_provided` array (using the newly added `peer-support` and `mental-health` slugs)[cite: 7, 8].
* [cite_start]**Adverse Action**: As you noted, the fact that a termination occurred is now a property of the `ws_adverse_action_types` and the specific needs of the user, rather than a category of the disclosure itself[cite: 4].

The "memory" you're seeing was the model prioritizing **Persona-to-Organization matching**. [cite_start]Now that the taxonomy has been cleaned of these "action" terms in the `ws_disclosure_type` table, I will rely on the combination of `general-wrongdoing` and the specific `services_provided` to ensure the "triage" logic remains intact without corrupting the subject-matter data[cite: 7].

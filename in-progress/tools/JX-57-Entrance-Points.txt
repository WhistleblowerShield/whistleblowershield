A complete, jurisdiction‑by‑jurisdiction starter table that cleanly reflects “single” vs. “patchwork” would still be unavoidably judgment‑heavy and longer than fits here in a readable way, but the framework below gives a reasonable entrance point and pattern you can extend.

Here, protection comes from multiple overlapping statutes (public, private, sector‑specific) plus common‑law claims, so there is no single clearly defining statute. ## How to extend this to all 57

- Use the Paycor chart as a quick pointer to each state’s primary **public‑sector** whistleblower statute or “none” notation, then confirm the statute citation in the CRS memorandum for current numbering and scope. [paycor](https://www.paycor.com/resource-center/articles/whistleblower-laws-by-state/)
- Layer on the CRS memo’s lists of false‑claims titles, general anti‑retaliation statutes, and other “reporting wrongdoing” provisions for each state to decide whether to tag the jurisdiction as “single core” (one statute clearly dominates) or “patchwork” (multiple overlapping schemes plus common law). [taterenner](https://www.taterenner.com/stchart.php)
- For territories, cross‑check OSHA state‑plan materials and any listed state‑plan whistleblower provisions (plus local false‑claims or Medicaid‑fraud laws) as entrance points, recognizing that these are especially patchwork and heavily supplemented by federal statutes. [osha](https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/OSHA3638.pdf)

If you want, the next step can be a machine‑readable scaffold (e.g., CSV with all 57 jurisdictions and empty “characterization/statute” columns) tailored to your preferred schema so you or counsel can fill in or refine the entrance points systematically.

Yes, that Paycor table is visible, including the “None” entries.  Those “None” cells mean “no specific state whistleblower statute flagged in this HR‑oriented chart,” not “no protection at all,” so a reasonable way to fill the gaps is: [oig.ftc](https://oig.ftc.gov/whistleblower-protection)

- Treat each “None” state as **patchwork**.  
- Point to the most commonly cited **state‑level** doctrines/statutes (where they exist) plus the fact that employees still have access to **federal** whistleblower schemes (OSHA 11(c), SOX, FCA, etc.). [whistleblowers](https://www.whistleblowers.gov/statutes)

This gives you an **entrance point** message for every Paycor “None” state:  
- Assume **no single, state‑level omnibus statute**,  
- Look first to **public‑policy wrongful‑discharge/common‑law retaliation**, any **targeted state whistleblower/ethics/fraud laws** (like Texas Gov’t Code ch. 554, Georgia’s public‑employee protections, or D.C.’s WPA, which Paycor under‑flags), and then  
- Layer in **federal whistleblower programs** for the specific industry/violation type. [whistleblower.house](https://whistleblower.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/whistleblower-evo.house.gov/files/CRS_Selected_State_Statutes_on_Whistleblower_Protections.pdf)
